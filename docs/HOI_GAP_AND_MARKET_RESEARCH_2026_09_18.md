**Status: Partial**

Hearts of Iron IV is still a live 2026 commercial title: paid expansions continue to sell national-focus trees together with research, industry, and diplomacy/naval systems, and concurrent-player spikes still line up with those DLC months rather than a collapse of the 2016 loop. [S1][S2] A non-Paradox HOI-like must land production, research/focus, land multi-front planning, basic supply, diplomacy/peace/occupation, intel hooks, OOB, and campaign save; commercial-depth designers, full naval task-force micro, and deep fuel can stay thinner. [S5] The realistic public bar is a long-polished Early Access 1.0 with a short fail-fast operational first session—not a vertical-slice demo and not Paradox DLC-depth. Victoria 3-style pops/markets and lockstep multiplayer kill indie scope; they are already explicit non-goals for an independent public launch.

### What still sells, and what must land
2024–2026 expansion copy still packages focus trees with industry, research, and diplomacy/naval systems instead of replacing those pillars. Country/focus packs stay flavor; expansions are the mechanics lane—Graveyard of Empires (4 Mar 2025, patch 1.16) is India/Iran/Iraq/Afghanistan focus trees, and Peace for Our Time is a Czechoslovakia focus pack. [S3]

| No Compromise, No Surrender | 20 Nov 2025 | $29.99 | Mostly Negative, 33% of 1,659 | Japan/China/Philippines focus trees; new doctrines; expanded factions; naval special projects; carrier combat |

Among those packs, the research/industry/focus expansion out-reviewed later doctrine/faction/naval and HQ packs, and its quarter generated higher Hearts of Iron revenue than the NCNS quarter, whose year-on-year rise was attributed mainly to Europa Universalis V and Bloodlines 2.

Tank designer, aircraft designer, and intelligence agencies are still DLC-gated (No Step Back, By Blood Alone, La Résistance); ship designer only became free with Man the Guns integration. Production, research/focus, land multi-front, a basic supply corridor, diplomacy/peace/occupation, intel, historical OOB, and save/autosave are the machine pillars to land. Division-designer depth, full naval designer/TF micro, and deep supply-hub/fuel economy can stay partial; a launch does not need commercial HOI designer tooling or HOI efficiency tables.

### Indie HOI-likes that actually launched—or failed
Solo-dev Age of History 3 launched 23 Oct 2024 at $9.99 as a cheap GSG in the vein of Europa Universalis and Hearts of Iron, with more than 4,000 concurrent players and 87% favorable reviews. Terra Invicta left Early Access on 5 Jan 2026. Rise of the White Sun is a live indie warlord-era GSG whose strength is staying comprehensible and manageable at any scale, with onboarding described as remarkably friendly versus typical GSG overload. SuperPower 3 (7 Oct 2022, Grand Strategy tag) remains Overwhelmingly Negative (10% of 982 reviews). The Kriegsspiel ~ 7 Years’ War Kickstarter failed on 25 Jul 2026 ($2,712 of $14,325, 62 backers). [S9]

### Where an indie can win
The commercial gap is incomplete launch systems plus inaccessible UI: Victoria 3’s warfare was a fail because it existed but was barebones against the player fantasy; grand strategy can be absolutely impenetrable, so accessibility work is required. A PDS GSG takes about five years versus about one year for a large DLC (EU4 has 37 DLC packs, Stellaris 28, Cities: Skylines 62). Rise of the White Sun’s contrast is no formal peace/war status and no war-score grind through an AI’s arbitrary insistence. After NCNS, naval AI underperformance—slow to react—was still a named pain point. [S10] Later HOI4 packs that leaned on doctrine/faction/naval/HQ depth also drew Mostly Negative Steam scores, while the industry/research expansion did not.

### Realistic launch bar vs DLC traps
Public 1.0 can be a long-polished Early Access product, not a vertical-slice demo. Terra Invicta’s January 2026 1.0 followed about three years of EA (from 2022), was already treated as launch-ready, and added scenarios, UI, tutorials, and a 2070 quick-start rather than a new core loop, with Cold War / post-nuclear scenarios, loyalty, environmental tipping points, and refugee mechanics left on a post-1.0 roadmap. [S11] Offworld Trading Company’s EA ran 14 months—about half of development; quality was framed as how many design–code–play–listen loops the game gets, and 1.0 two-week sales (23,607) nearly matched the EA launch (23,457). [S12]

Paradox itself deferred HOI4’s DLC-depth module designers until years after 1.0:

| Module | First shipped |
|---|---|
| Base game | 2016-06-06 [S13] |
| Ship Design (Man the Guns) | 2019-02-28 |
| Intelligence Agency (La Résistance) | 2020-02-25 |
| Tank Designer (No Step Back) | 2021-11-23 |
| Aircraft Designer (By Blood Alone) | 2022-09-27 |

Packing many interconnected GSG/wargame systems into one launch is a documented indie failure mode: Shadow Empire’s mix of 4X, logistics, politics, and simulation was called way too ambitious—years late, near burnout—with ocean/naval content left for post-launch (Oceania later shipped as expansion). [S14]

The first-session loop that actually works is a short operational scenario: pick a front, cut supply, hit timed objectives, fail fast and retry. Unity of Command 2 runs brief skirmishes—secondary goals in three or four turns, primary objectives in a generous 10–12—where victory is breaking through to a railroad junction that cuts supply. The contrast, not the success bar, is an opaque opening whose early stages last at least a dozen hours. [S15]

### Why V3 markets/pops and multiplayer kill indie scope
Victoria 3 pops are a combinatorial per-workplace simulation, not a few national manpower pools: every profession × culture × religion × workplace in each state is its own pop—many tens of thousands at once, with a design goal of 700+ regions and up to 100,000 groups that work, migrate, procreate, and agitate. [S16] Markets are a weekly open-economy snapshot with no goods stockpiles: production/consumption become buy/sell orders, then trade and prices, then wages/taxes/wealth, then the slate is wiped. Every state has a local price via Market Access Price Impact; pops and buildings transact at those local prices. A later World Market sits on top for autonomous trade only (exports vs imports), not pop/building purchases. [S17]

Paradox spent years after the 25 Oct 2022 launch rewriting that same economy: 1.5 (Nov 2023) added local prices/MAPI; 1.9 (2025) discarded trade from scratch as unreliable, fiddly, and inefficient since those local prices, deleting old trade routes for a World Market plus strategic national deals—while 2024 still shipped Sphere of Influence and Pivot of Empire. [S18] That is live-service staffing, not indie scope: a ~600-person group (average employees 587 in 2024, 649 in 2023, 654 in Q3 2025) with dedicated PDS sub-studios (Green, Teal, Purple, Black, Gold, plus Tinto) per active game, 22 expansions plus major free updates in 2024, and Victoria 3 described as poor aggregated financials until a multi-year investment paid off around the 1.9 / Charters of Commerce era. [S19]

Paradox GSGs use lockstep multiplayer: each client resimulates the whole game from a shared save plus commands, so any tiny divergence is an out-of-sync that stops the session and forces a rehost (for example a trade-route level 4 vs 5). Victoria 3 still ships official OOS diagnostics (folders under `Documents\Paradox Interactive\Victoria 3\oos`), with launch-era desyncs (Nov 2022) and later reports that after 1.13.4/1.13.8 multiplayer desyncs within ~2 years of play or becomes unplayable. [S20]

Full V3 markets/pops and a finished multiplayer product are already explicit non-goals for full-test / public-launch scope, alongside museum borders and a ~13k-province map; occupation/harvest is not a V3 goods market. [S21]
